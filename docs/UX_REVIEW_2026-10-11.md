# Product and UX review — 11 October 2026

The product has a strong personal identity and useful individual features. The
largest opportunity is to make them feel like one experience. The newer arrival,
Places, photo and conversation surfaces each bring their own navigation,
headings and actions. Studio has the same accumulation of controls.

The recommended direction is **less interface to interpret, more continuity,
and more room for the journey**. Keep the warm paper, editorial serif, personal
photographs and map. Apple-like quality here means a clear hierarchy, predictable
navigation, direct manipulation and truthful feedback. These criteria align
with [Apple’s design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles)
and [layout guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout);
the recommendations below are this review’s product judgments.

This original audit changed no behavior or trip content. The owner subsequently
authorized all findings and one-click fullscreen photo entry; the implementation
and verification record below supersedes the proposal-only status. The later
11 October clarification also recorded below supersedes the initial generic
all-photo-fullscreen interpretation: map circles/right-hand reel thumbnails
select in the existing sidebar/card, and the large selected/lead image opens
fullscreen. Old map popups close before switching days.

## Detailed recommendations

### U01 — Make the phone city screen one coherent card

**Observed:** After choosing Prague, the map’s arrival card says “5 Oct–11 Oct /
In Prague” with “Replay arrival” and “View photos.” Immediately below it, the
persistent stop card repeats the date, Prague and “Photos · 14.” The bottom
picker names Prague again. The header also has the whole-trip Replay action.
The competing cards consume useful map space and make the next action less clear.

**Recommendation:** Integrate the arrival status into the existing stop card.
During travel, show the route/progress and Skip there; after arrival, resolve to
the city name, date and one Photos action. Put Replay arrival in a secondary
action. Preserve the single bottom stop picker. Provide direct contextual
access to Places alongside the city’s content; currently the journey-wide
Places action is in the chooser after the 29-stop list, although map pins offer
another entry.

**Acceptance:** At 393 px, the resting city screen has one date/title block and
one album action. Arrival, photos and Places preserve the same selected stop.
The layout still works with no photos, no Places, long titles and daily events.

Owners: shared journey template, `app.js`, `mobile-ux.js`, `mobile.css`,
`arrival-chapter.css`.

### U02 — Give desktop content more room and reduce competing emphasis

**Observed:** The desktop viewer repeats branding in the header and repeats the
trip title in the left panel and above the map. Heading East’s map toolbar has
five similarly prominent dark buttons. Orange photo/place counts look more
urgent than simple totals warrant. At 1280 × 720, the map itself starts around
y208 and is about 594 px wide; the right-hand arrival/photo surfaces further
compete for attention.

**Recommendation:** Keep the established days-left, map-center, details-right
layout. Use one principal trip title, a compact map toolbar and quiet utility
controls. Treat counts as metadata. Within the existing right sidebar, give
the current photograph and story precedence over repeated section labels and
instructions. Align button shapes, selected states, spacing and type between
the angular legacy viewer, rounded Places surfaces and Studio.

**Acceptance:** Fit route and route detail remain discoverable; keyboard labels
remain explicit; there is a clear visual distinction between the current view,
an action and metadata. Check 1024/1280/1440 px, including long trip titles.

### U03 — Make Places respect the context that opened it

**Observed:** Select Prague, then open “Places · 20.” The list starts with
Luzern and Freiburg. “This stop only” is unchecked. Enabling it returns six
Prague places. The control works, but the initial result makes users reconstruct
their current context.

**Recommendation:** Entry from a focused city should default to “Prague · 6”;
entry from the whole-trip overview should default to “All places · 20.” Expose
that scope visibly and preserve deliberate subsequent choices. A map-pin entry
should return to its scoped list. Avoid silently changing a scope the visitor
has intentionally selected.

**Acceptance:** City → Places shows that city first; switching to all places is
one action; Back restores the originating result and scroll; empty current-city
results offer “See all places.” Group and TBD filters remain respected.

Source: `dist/assets/places-panel.js:55`, `:80`, `:326`, `:380`.

### U04 — Keep conversation navigation inside the presenting photo collection

**Observed:** Prague photo 5 of 14 becomes “Photo 35 of 44” when Comments opens.
The comments surface has its own previous/next controls. Source confirms that
these use the journey-wide photo list. A selected map group introduces a third
collection, such as 1 of 9. The numbers are internally explainable, but the
visitor has not requested a change of collection.

**Recommendation:** Pass the presenting collection and current selection into
Comments. Preserve the album or map-group count, ordering and route filter.
Alternatively, keep Comments attached to one photo and remove its independent
photo navigation. Label any explicit transition to all trip photos.

**Acceptance:** Album photo 5 of 14 remains 5 of 14 in Comments; arrows cannot
silently leave the collection; closing returns to the same photo; per-photo
drafts and identity remain intact. Test last-photo boundaries and map groups.

Source: `dist/assets/photo-comments.js:155`, `:355` and the shared `photos`
provider in `dist/assets/app.js:2251`.

### U05 — Use one vocabulary and honor blank captions everywhere

**Observed:** Prague is Stop 7 throughout navigation but “Day 7” inside a place’s
facts. An uncaptioned photograph appears as “Trip photograph from Prague” in
Comments, while the photo viewer correctly uses its timestamp. The comment
title function falls back to accessibility alt text. Video intake also lists
base day titles such as “Brienz, Interlaken & Mürren” where the edited family
journal calls that day “The Marathoner Tag.”

**Recommendation:** Use the shared day/stop and resolved-title helpers in every
surface. Keep accessibility descriptions in accessibility attributes. For an
uncaptioned photo, show a neutral photo position or a readable capture date,
such as “7 Oct · 18:01,” without inventing a title, venue or memory. A Back
label like “Prague” is more recognizable than “Stop 7” alone.

**Acceptance:** No visible alt-text fallback; matching event names in viewer,
Places, Comments and Studio; identical date conventions; owner-written captions
and manually saved place links retain precedence.

Source: `dist/assets/places-panel.js:150`,
`dist/assets/photo-comments.js:139`, `studio/plan-extras.js`.

### U06 — Make place reviews part of the place, with a quiet empty state

**Observed:** Naše maso has both a “No group reviews yet” action and a Reviews
item that looks like a peer of Overview/Photos. Reviews opens a separate large
dialog with only “One for next time.” Overview and Photos behave as in-panel
tabs. The extra modal supplies little information and gives visually similar
controls different navigation behavior.

**Recommendation:** Keep reviews in the same detail surface, with consistent
tab behavior. When there are no reviews, use a short inline state and remove
the duplicate prominent zero-review entry. Keep a compact rating summary when
reviews exist. Preserve the distinction between owner-curated group reviews
and visitor photo conversations; do not imply that photo access grants a place
reviewer role.

**Acceptance:** No nested modal solely to explain zero reviews; Back and Escape
have consistent meanings; the populated review distribution remains available.

### U07 — Let a saved place feel complete without trip photos or reviews

**Observed:** Naše maso is already selected and centered on the map, yet “Show
on map” remains the primary button. Below its facts, an empty trip-photo section
says “No linked trip photos in the selected route group,” even though Heading
East has no route groups. On the phone, the venue image and zero-review UI push
useful place facts/actions below the initially visible sheet.

**Recommendation:** Make recenter a quiet action when the selected place is
already visible. Surface its name, useful description and Maps link sooner.
Show the trip-photo section when there are explicit links; otherwise omit it
or use “No trip photos linked yet” where an explanation is useful. Distinguish
“Our photos” from credited “Venue photos” with concise labels and visible
provenance. Do not add automatic nearby-photo associations.

**Acceptance:** A saved venue with one credited image and no reviews/linked
photos looks intentional. No group terminology appears without a group. Image
credits stay reachable and no saved place is presented as an actual visit.

Source: `dist/assets/places-panel.js:150`, `:153`.

### U08 — Organize Studio around tasks people recognize

**Observed:** Clicking the top-level Places button opens a section inside Trip
plan & media and highlights that broader mode. Private videos are in Trip plan
& media while photo intake is in Photos. The planner includes trip facts,
curated places, route destinations, people, groups, video, cover and Replay.
The header Save/Ready controls compete with a second plan-save bar.

**Recommendation:** Use a stable task structure such as **Trip, Media, Places,
Routes**, with stories within Trip and advanced Replay controls disclosed there.
Make Places a real selected workspace. Let photos and videos share the Media
library/intake entry, keeping their different processing requirements underneath.
Keep one consistent save action and a distinct review-for-sharing action. Expose
the existing autosaved recovery state honestly; this does not require changing
the save/publishing contract in the same iteration.

**Acceptance:** Navigation selection matches the visible editor; adding a clip
is discoverable from the media library; the same Save action handles the current
draft; no loss of recovery, review/discard or conflict reconciliation.

Source: `studio/index.html`, `studio/studio.js:1249` and nearby Places handler.

### U09 — Make batch editing a contact sheet first

**Observed:** At 1280 × 800, Select multiple expands the journey chooser,
full-width New trip action, filter/search fields, selection controls, action
form and upload disclosure above the grid. The first thumbnail row starts near
the bottom of the viewport. At 393 × 852 the photos are below the initial view.
The feature saves clicks once reached but makes visual selection feel secondary.

**Recommendation:** Put a compact filter bar above the grid and a contextual
action bar below it. Show the selection count, target action and Undo near the
photos. Replace “Apply to 6 photos” with an outcome-specific label such as “Move
6 photos to Prague” or “Move 6 photos to Trash.” Keep the useful outside-filter
selection count explicit and easy to inspect/clear.

**Acceptance:** Photos are visible immediately after entering selection mode
on desktop and phone. Controls stay reachable while scrolling. Selection
survives filters without hidden surprises; existing guarded Undo is preserved.

Source: `studio/photo-batch.css`, `studio/photo-batch-ui.js`,
`studio/index.html:73`.

### U10 — Choose photographs visually

**Observed:** The cover selector lists many IDs such as `family-img-1469` and
long upload hashes. Place-photo linking includes thumbnails, but the labels
also fall back to IDs, and its search does not include the same capture-date
vocabulary as the main photo contact sheet. The Places form presents 29 stop
checkboxes before later editing sections.

**Recommendation:** Reuse a visual photo chooser for cover selection and
explicit place links. Include day/stop and capture time, with filenames in a
secondary inspector when needed. Let the cover focal point be placed directly
on the existing desktop/phone previews. Use a compact multi-select for stop
assignment. Keep the underlying IDs and explicit saved associations unchanged.

**Acceptance:** The owner can choose a recognizable photograph and set its
cover crop without learning an ID. Linking is manual, searchable by familiar
metadata and ordered. Empty captions remain empty.

Source: `studio/place-editor.js:16`, cover controls in `studio/studio.js`
and `studio/index.html`.

### U11 — Turn Ready to share into a short decision, with details available

**Observed:** The family journey reports “Local checks passed · 9 review items
left.” Three source-state boxes and explanatory paragraphs precede individual
route/photo review cards. On phone the first issue is near the bottom of the
initial view. The primary handoff action is available while review items remain;
the text does explain this, but the page needs substantial reading.

**Recommendation:** Lead with a concise outcome: **Saved locally · No publishing
blockers · 9 choices to review**. Group repeated route items. Separate required
fixes from optional decisions and expose details on demand. Use “Review for
sharing” for the entry action, then an outcome-specific handoff label. Keep
technical revisions and the operational checklist inside the copyable agent
handoff. Keep “Live version not checked” honest; only a verified receipt could
justify saying the current draft is live.

**Acceptance:** In one screen, the owner can tell what needs action, what can be
kept as-is and what happens next. A changed revision invalidates old decisions.
Preparing a handoff remains read-only and never masquerades as publication.

Source: `studio/ready-to-share.js:76`, `:87`, `:115`.

### U12 — Give private-video preparation a visible lifecycle

**Observed in source:** Import displays “This may take a few minutes” while a
single request performs upload/preparation. There is no visible staged progress
or cancel control in the form. The unload guard checks photo upload state but
does not include `importingVideo`. No real clip was imported to reproduce an
interruption in this review.

**Recommendation:** Show truthful stages such as transferring, preparing and
ready for preview. Use an indeterminate progress indicator when a percentage is
unavailable. Define cancellation and navigation behavior, and keep completed
imports discoverable if the owner changes workspace. Explain that the prepared
clip is local with a short status, not repeated infrastructure terminology.

**Acceptance:** A long operation never looks abandoned; navigation cannot hide
its outcome; failures retain the selected title/day and offer a clear retry;
originals remain preserved. Verify with a synthetic clip and interruption tests.

Source: `studio/studio.js:1228`, `:1235`, `:1745`.

### U13 — Make incomplete journeys purposeful

**Observed:** The fresh empty draft offers “Relive the trip,” then a world map
with “No location added for this day.” “All photos · 0” opens three “Read the
story” cards even though their stories are blank. The underlying capabilities
are inherited correctly; the choices promise more content than exists.

**Recommendation:** Preserve shared capabilities with content-aware presentation.
Lead an empty preview with “Your journey is taking shape” and its dates. Make
the day list useful, put empty media/Replay actions in a secondary or clearly
unavailable state, and call a blank day “View day” rather than “Read the story.”
In Studio, offer concrete first actions such as Add a stop or Add photos instead
of filling the workspace with an inactive location inspector.

**Acceptance:** Each enabled primary action has a meaningful destination; adding
content naturally reveals the existing shared controls. No invented itinerary,
coordinates, story or photo is required to make an empty trip look intentional.

### U14 — Clear stale publication feedback when no photo is selected

**Reproduction:** In Studio Photos, start on a published family photograph,
then select the newly generated empty draft. Its inspector correctly says
“Choose a photo,” but the lower status still reads “Photo assets published ·
edits appear after the next site deployment.” The no-photo path resets the form
and returns before clearing that status.

**Recommendation:** Clear all photo-specific status, order and action text when
there is no selected photo. Show publication status only for the actual current
asset. This is a small, concrete correctness fix independent of a redesign.

**Acceptance:** Populated → empty journey, filtered-out selection and last-photo
removal never retain another photo’s state. Publication claims remain tied to
the current asset.

Source: `studio/studio.js:304–332`.

### U15 — Separate plans from memories through language

**Observed:** Heading East includes future and TBD stops, but its cover’s primary
action is “Relive the trip.” The catalog displays 10,268 km without the map
panel’s “includes TBD” qualifier. The Places heading says “Places we found”
while all 20 saved venues are marked “Saved for later.” Each individual label
has context elsewhere, but the combined impression is more definitive than
the underlying records.

**Recommendation:** Use neutral entry wording such as “Explore the journey” for
mixed planned/recorded trips. Qualify estimated totals in the catalog or omit
them there. Prefer “Saved places” when the set is entirely saved. Distinguish
confirmed itinerary from actual visits; do not infer completion or a visit from
the date, proximity or a photograph.

**Acceptance:** A first-time visitor can distinguish recorded travel, future
plans and open ideas from the initial screen without opening a legend.

### U16 — Finish motion, map symbols and Replay as one system

**Observed/source:** The map combines route endpoints, numbered signposts,
place clusters, large food markers and photo clusters. Arrival runs when a new
stop is selected; cached desktop images deliberately replay a 650 ms reveal
(350 ms for the full viewer). Replay has usable playback controls and a slider,
but seeking still relies on a largely unnamed track.

**Recommendation:** Compare a quieter density treatment and motion timing
locally. Establish a clear order for selected content, nearby content and
background markers. Keep counts visually distinct from stop numbers. Preserve
the approved short, interruptible arrival and existing reduced-motion behavior;
test whether repeated image reveals help or slow rapid browsing. Add named
chapter previews/ticks to the existing Replay timeline as a separate proposal.

**Acceptance:** Fast stop/photo switching keeps pace with intent, selected
content stays legible, and a visitor can seek a named stop without guessing.
Do not reintroduce photos into deliberately map-only Replay. This review did
not measure frame rate or certify a motion-performance defect.

Source: `dist/assets/atlas-utils.js:275–312`, `dist/assets/app.js:1253`,
`content/templates/journey.html:255–274`.

## Proposed flow

**Visitor:** Choose a journey → choose a day/city → browse its story, photos or
saved places → open one item → return to the same selection and collection.
Whole-trip Replay is a separate deliberate mode. The UI should make the current
trip, stop, filter and collection apparent without repeating them in every card.

**Owner:** Open a trip → work in Trip, Media, Places or Routes → see the current
draft/save state → preview → review for sharing → hand the concrete checked
draft to the agent. Advanced provenance and processing details stay available
where they are relevant. Save, recovery and publishing retain their distinct
meanings.
