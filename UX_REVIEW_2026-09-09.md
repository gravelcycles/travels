# Journey Atlas — click-through UX review

Reviewed 9 September 2026. This is an audit and proposed implementation brief; no product changes were made or published during this review.

The strongest direction is a beautiful atlas that becomes a family film. The ingredients already exist: real photographs, carefully drawn routes, a distinctive paper-and-ink style, and Replay. The next improvement should make those pieces feel like one continuous experience.

## What was actually checked

- Published catalog, journey introduction, About dialog, all 14 family journal days, route selection, map fit/day controls, and a shared-location marker.
- Every one of the 14 family Replay chapters; play/pause, keyboard timeline seeking, explore/return, departure, and the completion state.
- All-photos overview, the 21-photo Day 6 album, first/last-image states, continuation to Day 7, the one-photo final day, and returning to the journal. This was not an individual visual review of all 99 photographs.
- All 22 days across the four sample journeys, journey switching, a photo-free album, and its skip-to-photos action.
- Desktop at 1280 × 800 and 1280 × 720; phone at 390 × 844; the introduction at 320 × 568.
- Local Studio: Photos, Route drawing, Trip plan & cover, Curated Replay moments, Day copy, and opening/canceling New trip. No content, route, upload, trash, or save operations were performed.
- Browser Back/Forward, a fresh reload, and a direct Day 6 link. The browser already had remembered photo access; initial password entry and logout were not retested. No console warnings/errors appeared in the inspected log.

Reduced-motion settings, real-device touch/pinch behavior, offline/slow-network failures, a full screen-reader session, and Studio persistence were not exercised. No build or test suite was run for this documentation-only audit. Other local code changes appeared during the review; public findings below describe the deployed site observed in this session and should be reconciled with that work before implementation.

## Fix first

### B1 · Phone Replay controls move when the chapter changes

**Confirmed on the published site.** At 390 × 844, a chapter with a photograph puts the player controls near the bottom of the screen. Seeking to Day 3, which has no selected Replay photo, moves the entire control bar roughly 238 px upward and leaves a large empty area below it. The Play button and timeline are moving targets.

**Reproduce:** Relive the trip → pause → seek between Day 2 and Day 3.

**Fix:** give the player a stable viewport layout, with a reserved control row and a flexible scene area. A route-only chapter should expand the map into the available space. Avoid making control placement depend on photo/caption height.

**Acceptance:** controls remain in the same position across photo, route-only, rest-day, loading, and completion states on normal and short phones. The current photo and essential controls remain reachable without competing scroll areas.

### B2 · Close and route-detail controls collide with map controls

**Confirmed on the published site.** The desktop photo viewer places its close button over the map's zoom-in control. Replay has the same collision, including on mobile. On a 390 px phone, selecting the Day 6 Brienz → Interlaken ferry opens a route inspector that also overlaps the map controls and the lower edge of the day navigator.

**Fix:** reserve distinct areas for dismissal, map zoom, day navigation, and route information. Position route information in a compact bottom card above the legend, or another reserved area, and use the actual free map rectangle when framing a route.

**Acceptance:** visible controls do not intersect at 320, 390, and 1280 px widths. Attribution remains legible and reachable. Clicking the visible zoom-in control cannot close a viewer.

### B3 · Browser history and the displayed journey state disagree

**Confirmed on the published site.** Catalog → journey → Day 6 album → continue to Day 7 → browser Back returns to the catalog. Forward returns to the introduction, losing the open album. Separately, opening `?day=family-d6`, clicking Next day, and reloading returns to Day 6 because the URL never changed.

**Fix:** synchronize the selected day and open viewer with history. Back should close the current layer before leaving the journey; Forward should restore it. Preserve the photo index and relevant scroll position. Replace the current entry while stepping within an album rather than adding 99 history entries.

**Acceptance:** copied URLs, reload, Back/Forward, the visible day, and the selected photo agree. An album entered through All photos has a clear return to that overview.

### B4 · Studio Replay photograph options are blank

**Confirmed in local Studio and its rendered DOM.** Trip plan & cover → Curated Replay moments → Moment 1's Photograph control contains six photo options with empty labels. The chosen photograph also looks blank. The source renders only `p.caption`; blank captions are intentional for this project.

**Fix:** reuse a visual photo picker with thumbnails, day, photo number, and capture time. Provide a filename fallback for accessible labels. Keep captions optional and do not generate prose to repair a selector.

**Acceptance:** every visible photograph can be identified and selected without requiring a caption. Existing selections remain visible after changing a moment's day or hiding a referenced photo.

### B5 · Shared-location markers do not offer a choice of days

**Confirmed by clicking the Luzern group and inspecting the handler.** A marker representing Days 1, 5, 7, and 13 picks one day based on the currently selected day. The map then filters the group down to that day, so repeated taps cannot browse the other days as the grouping suggests.

**Fix:** tapping a group should open a small day chooser with date and title. A single-day marker can continue to select directly.

**Acceptance:** each represented day is reachable from the marker using touch or keyboard, without returning to Fit route between choices.

### B6 · The mobile samples page has no visible route back to the catalog

**Confirmed on the published site.** The desktop Journey catalog link is hidden on mobile. The samples logo links back to `demo.html`, so it only reloads the samples page.

**Fix:** retain a compact catalog/back link on mobile or point the brand home link at the catalog.

**Acceptance:** a visitor arriving directly at the samples URL can reach the real journeys without using browser history.

### B7 · Studio's daily-lead wording no longer matches this trip's viewer

**Observed in Studio; behavior confirmed in source.** Photos still offers “Use as lead photo” and says the album order is independent from the lead choice. The public journal and album overview deliberately use photo 1, while curated Replay uses each moment's explicit photo. The daily lead therefore does not change those surfaces for this curated trip.

**Fix:** clarify where this setting applies, or remove it from workflows where it has no effect. Retain the requested behavior that day previews open photo 1. Automatic, uncurated Replay still has a separate lead fallback; account for that use.

**Acceptance:** every exposed cover/lead control states which visible result it changes.

## Make the experience cleaner

1. **Give each screen one clear identity.** On desktop, the brand is repeated within itself, and the long trip title appears in both the sidebar and map header. Keep one trip heading, compact date/day facts, and a clear active-day label. Use a single visitor-facing name for Relive/Trip replay/Replay. Keep sample and technical explanation links secondary.

2. **Shrink the phone map toolbar.** The site header plus trip title and four equal-weight buttons consume about 224 px before the map begins at 390 × 844. Keep the current day and navigation close to the map; make Relive and Photos easy to find; treat framing controls as compact map tools. Keep the existing Days/Map/Journal synchronization.

3. **Put day navigation within easy reach.** Day 6 has nine visible legs before its Previous/Next day controls. Preserve the requested always-expanded itinerary, but place a compact day navigator near the heading or keep it reachable while scrolling. Use consistent transport icons, quieter repeated metadata, and readable labels rather than shrinking the text further.

4. **Make the phone photo viewer spacious.** Its lower section currently divides a small map and day/thumbnail controls into narrow columns. Let the photograph dominate; put filmstrip navigation in one horizontal row and make location details an expandable map panel. Preserve exact photo locations and the existing two-second camera transition when that map is open. Provide a clear return to All photos when that was the entry point.

5. **Clean the remaining copy and metadata.** Existing photos show dates like “18 Aug · 08:02”, while uploads can show “2026-08-26 · 08:20”. Format them consistently. The About dialog's planning instructions are editor-oriented; concentrate it on who traveled and how to browse. Improve generic or stale accessibility descriptions separately from optional visible captions.

6. **Simplify Studio's primary choices.** Replay editing is buried under Trip plan & cover, and the planner presents both the global Save locally and a separate Save trip plan locally action. Make the current save scope explicit. Prefer photo thumbnails and human-readable labels to long internal IDs. Keep the cover crop previews and route review safeguards.

## The most promising delight work

### D1 · Give Replay the rhythm of a small family film

This is the signature feature worth concentrating on. Five published chapters currently show no photo: Days 3, 8, 9, 11, and 14. Their albums now have 13, 4, 2, 4, and 1 visible photographs respectively. That is an editorial opportunity, not a request to fill every chapter automatically.

Build a deliberate sequence from existing photos: establish the journey on the map, let the route draw, then give a selected photograph a larger moment. Day 6 could move from the lake journey to the group-on-the-boat image and later the mountain scene. Use the photograph's reviewed location when entering a photo scene; retain route framing while showing a travel leg. Avoid camera flights just for decoration.

Keep the existing 2× default and optional blank copy. Express the story through image selection, scale, transitions, and pacing. Let the final family photograph and a settled view of the complete route provide a warmer ending than only “Journey complete”.

### D2 · Make movement between views feel continuous

Animate the selected thumbnail into its full photo, retain the selected day and scroll position on return, and transition the current-day indicator with the user's action. A short, restrained transition can explain where the user went. Respect reduced motion and never delay interaction for an animation.

### D3 · Turn the Replay timeline into a browsable memory strip

Show day/moment names and a preview of the selected photograph when seeking. A person should be able to find the boat ride or mountain day by recognition instead of guessing where it sits on an unlabeled slider. Use the existing curated moments and stored photographs.

### D4 · Finish albums with a clear handoff to tomorrow

The orange Next day card already works. Strengthen that successful pattern: show the next day's existing title and thumbnail at the album boundary, keep the action in the same location, and give the final day an intentional return to the whole journey. Avoid an ambiguous disabled arrow as the main end-of-album signal.

## Suggested implementation order

1. **Reliable navigation:** B1–B6, especially stable Replay controls, collision-free maps, and history restoration.
2. **Clearer surfaces:** remove repeated headings, simplify the phone map toolbar and viewer, bring day navigation forward, and reconcile Studio's labels/save scopes.
3. **Signature experience:** fix the Studio photo picker, curate Replay against the current 99-photo album, then add scene transitions, visual seeking, and a more personal ending.

Keep the compact catalog rows, existing family imagery, chronological album entry, optional blank prose, expanded travel legs, transport distinctions, reviewed geometry, and private-photo access. These are established product choices. Recheck the affected public and sample flows after each implementation pass, rather than treating older handoff claims as current behavior.
